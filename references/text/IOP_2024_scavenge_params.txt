Analysis of the interactive effects of scavenge parameters on
spark ignition two-stroke diesel engines
Tingting Sun1, Shangxue Wang1,3, Xuesong Zhang2, Chengwei Zhao1, Xiaoyan
Zhang and Hongru Zhu1
1 North China Engine Research Institute (Tianjin), Tianjin 300400, China
2 Shanxi Diesel Engine Industry Co., Ltd, Datong, Shanxi, 037036, China
3 Corresponding author’s e-mail: wsx173@163.com
Abstract. The scavenging efficiency of a two-stroke diesel engine affects the formation of the
mixture in the engine cylinder, which in turn affects the engine's performance. A
one-dimensional simulation model of a two-stroke diesel engine is established using
GT-POWER, and the model with original experimental data is calibrated as boundary
conditions. Firstly, the influence of crankcase compression ratio on engine performance of the
two-stroke engine was investigated to obtain an appropriate crankcase compression ratio; Then,
based on the optimization index of scavenging efficiency, the optimal combination of engine
air port structure and timing is obtained through orthogonal optimization calculation method.
To achieve higher scavenging efficiency, when the phase of the air port remains unchanged, a
scavenging port width ratio of 1.0 and an exhaust port width ratio of 0.8 can achieve a
scavenging efficiency of 97.2%; When the air port size remains unchanged, the engine
scavenging efficiency reaches 96.44% when the scavenging port timing is 123° CA and the
exhaust port timing is 89° CA.
1. Introduction
Two-stroke engines have the advantages of simple structure, compactness, lightweight, high power
output, and low cost, making them widely used in civil and military aviation power devices. However,
there are also many structural defects, such as short ventilation time, short circuits during the
scavenging process, and high carbon and hydrogen emissions, which make it difficult to meet the
needs of engine power and economy [1-2]. Therefore, a large number of researchers have conducted
extensive research on the matching of the ventilation system and the optimization of the airport
structure in two-stroke engines. Bai Jingyuan et al. have optimized the envelope angle and elevation
angle of the scavenging port in two-stroke engines, and have experimentally verified the calculation
results [3]; Zhang Shuolu et al. investigated the effect of scavenging pressure on the scavenging
efficiency of two-stroke engines [4]. The research results indicate that the performance of two-stroke
engines is greatly affected by the valve timing effect. Therefore, this article focuses on the working
characteristics of a spark ignition two-stroke diesel engine and uses GT power software to establish a
one-dimensional simulation model of the two-stroke diesel engine. By studying the interactive effects
of crankcase compression ratio, airport size, and intake and exhaust phase on the engine's air exchange
effect, the scavenging process and efficiency are optimized to improve the overall performance of the
engine.

2. Engine simulation model establishment and validation
2.1 Model establishment
The cylinder working process of a two-stroke engine includes the flow field movement of gas in the
cylinder and the process of being compressed and ignited, which combines very complex physical and
chemical changes. The performance calculation theory of two-stroke engines is based on the
conservation of mass, momentum, energy, and composition. Using one-dimensional flow inside the
tube and cylinder content product method, each discrete unit transfers and exchanges mass,
momentum, and energy through connected boundaries [5-7].
Aiming at the power requirements of diesel-electric hybrid power devices as the performance
design goal, the performance simulation calculation of the entire engine is carried out. Through
performance calculation, the overall performance parameters of the entire machine and the
performance indicators of each system are determined, which serve as the input boundary for
subsystem selection and matching design. A one-dimensional performance simulation model of a
spark ignition two-stroke single-cylinder diesel engine was established based on the engine of a certain
drone as a reference structure, as shown in Figure 1.
Figure 1. One-dimensional performance simulation model of a spark ignition two-stroke diesel
engine.
According to the fluid flow process: air passes through the intake pipe and intake duct from the
intake boundary, then enters the crankcase. With the movement of the piston, it enters the cylinder
through the scavenging port, and high-temperature exhaust gas is discharged from the exhaust port
after combustion in the cylinder [8-9]. The model is divided into four modules, namely the intake
module, cylinder module, crankcase module, and exhaust module.
2.2 Model validation
The cylinder pressure curve of the original engine is obtained through experiments, and the
one-dimensional simulation model of a two-stroke diesel engine established by GT-POWER software
is calibrated. The comparison results between simulation values and experimental values are shown in
Figure 2. After calibration, the simulation and experimental data values of the model in the
compression and expansion stages are consistent. During the combustion process, there is a certain
error due to the calculation error of the heat transfer process, but the maximum error is less than 5%.
The simulation model can be used for the next step of simulation calculation.

4. Research on the influence of crankcase compression ratio
The two-stroke engine in this study adopts intake crankcase turbocharging, which affects the
scavenging efficiency of the two-stroke engine by the compression ratio of the crankcase, thereby
affecting the engine's power and economy. Therefore, exploring the influence of different crankcase
compression ratios on the engine under all operating conditions provides a reference for further
optimizing engine crankcase design. Considering the actual application range of the crankcase
compression ratio, the exploration range here is 1.2 ~ 2.4
The effect of crankcase compression ratio on scavenging under engine calibration conditions (5500
r/min) is shown in Figure 4. As the compression ratio of the crankcase increases, the inflation
efficiency in the engine cylinder also increases. However, because the scavenging efficiency is also
affected by the capture rate in the engine cylinder, as the crankcase compression ratio increases, the
scavenging efficiency of the engine first increases and then slightly decreases. When the compression
ratio of the crankcase is 1.5, the scavenging efficiency reaches a maximum of 95.14%.
Figure 4. Effect of crankcase compression ratio on scavenging.
The effect of the crankcase compression ratio on the engine under all operating conditions is shown
in Figures 5 - 6. As the compression ratio of the crankcase increases, the torque of the engine shows an
increasing trend. When the compression ratio of the crankcase is 1.2, due to the poor scavenging effect
of the engine, there is less fresh charge entering the cylinder, resulting in poor combustion of the
engine. Therefore, the higher the speed, the greater the torque decrease. But when the crankcase
compression ratio increases to 1.8, continuing to increase the crankcase compression ratio will result
in a smaller change in engine torque, but it will lead to a rapid increase in engine fuel consumption.
When the engine speed is low, the compression ratio of the engine's crankcase is relatively small,
allowing more fresh charge to remain inside the cylinder for the same scavenging time, resulting in
full combustion. Therefore, at low speeds, a smaller compression ratio of the crankcase will obtain
greater torque and lower fuel consumption. Therefore, considering both power and economic factors, a
crankcase compression ratio of 1.8 can achieve relatively good engine performance.

5. Research on the influence of airport width and timing
5.1 Analysis of the interaction of airport width
The scavenging efficiency of a two-stroke engine is also affected by the parameters of the engine
airport. Based on the previous research to obtain a better engine crankcase compression ratio, further
explore the impact of engine air port width on engine performance.
The choice of airport width affects the face value of the airport during the scavenging stage,
determining the completeness of the scavenging process, and thus has a significant impact on the
performance of two-stroke engines. The positions of the engine's scavenging and exhaust ports are
shown in Figure 7.
A study was conducted on the interactive effects of sweeping and exhaust port width across the
entire operating range of the engine. For the convenience of exploration, using the original airport size
as a benchmark and keeping the height unchanged to ensure that the valve timing remains unchanged,
the width of the airport is multiplied by a coefficient, which is manifested as the change in the area of
the scavenging port, while keeping the position and size of the other airport unchanged. This method is
used to explore the impact of airport width on engine performance. Considering the actual proportion
of airport width on the circumference of the cylinder, the airport width ratio coefficient is set between
0.8 and 1.2. The scavenging process of the engine is influenced by both the scavenging port and the
exhaust port, but the joint influence of the two port sizes is not yet clear. Therefore, the interaction
effect of airport width is explored.
Figure 7. Schematic diagram of air outlet position.
Figure 8 shows the comprehensive effect of scavenging and exhaust port height on the scavenging
efficiency of a two-stroke diesel engine under a rated speed of 5500 r/min. There is a significant
interaction effect between the width of the intake and exhaust ports on the scavenging efficiency. The
larger the width coefficient of the airport, the larger the sweeping surface during the sweeping stage.
When the width coefficient of the scavenging port is 1.0 and the width coefficient of the exhaust port
is 0.8, the scavenging efficiency reaches the maximum value of 97.2%.
Figure 8. Scavenge efficiency of different area ratios of sweeping and exhaust ports.

5.2 Analysis of the interaction effect of airport timing
The scavenging and exhaust ports of a two-stroke diesel engine are arranged in a circular pattern on
the cylinder liner, and the opening and closing timing of the diesel engine's airports is controlled by
the movement of the piston. The position of the sweeping is moved and ports on the cylinder liner are
exhausted, and the valve timing of the diesel engine will change accordingly. The optimal position is
selected, which is the optimal valve timing, to achieve the best performance of the diesel engine.
This article analyzes the variation of diesel engine scavenging efficiency with valve timing by
changing the intake and exhaust phase, that is, changing the position of the scavenging port on the
cylinder liner. The size of the valve timing angle directly determines the performance of a diesel
engine. Each model has its optimal valve timing. Original machine gas distribution phase: the opening
angle of the scavenging port is 124° CA, and the closing angle of the intake port is 236° CA; The
opening angle of the exhaust port is 87° CA, and the closing angle of the exhaust port is 273° CA.
Considering the actual position range of the airport on the cylinder, the timing deviation of the airport
is set between ± 2° CA.
The position of the scavenging port on the cylinder liner is moved and a set of scavenging opening
angles / scavenging closing angles are calculated. The flow area is input at different opening, and
closing times of the scavenging port are input into a one-dimensional simulation model, and the
performance of the diesel engine is calculated under different intake phases through simulation.
The timing of the airports affects the overlap angle of the engine's airports, which in turn affects the
engine's scavenging process. However, the impact of the timing match between the two on scavenging
is not yet clear. Analyze the interaction between different air port timing under the rated speed of 5500
r/min for the original machine structure. Figure 9 shows the effect of the timing of the scavenging and
exhaust ports on the scavenging efficiency. The exhaust port is delayed in opening, and the free
exhaust stage is shortened. When the scavenging port is at 125° CA and the exhaust port is open at 89°
CA, the scavenging efficiency reaches its maximum value of 96.4%.
Figure 9. Scavenge efficiency at different opening times of the sweeping and exhaust ports.
6. Conclusion

engine was established using GT-POWER software, and the model was calibrated using the original
cylinder pressure curve. Then, the engine performance was simulated using the model. The specific
conclusions are as follows:
(1)The scavenging efficiency of a two-stroke engine is influenced by both the feed ratio and the
capture rate;
(2)For a two-stroke engine with crankcase turbocharging, a larger crankcase compression ratio is
not necessarily better, but rather a comprehensive consideration of the actual operating conditions of
